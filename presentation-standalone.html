06  ·  Frequency analysis

Fourier and its Limits

Why the Fourier transform is not enough for images.

The Fourier Transform

Decomposes a signal into pure frequencies — ideal for stationary signals.

F(u,v) = ∫∫ f(x,y) · e−j 2π(ux+vy) dx dy
VS

Major limitation

No spatial localization: we know that a frequency exists — but not where.

Images are non-stationary: edges, textures, flat regions — all coexist spatially.

We need a tool that sees both frequency and position: wavelets.

09  ·  Proposed approach

Modeling DWT-PVS & LWT-PVS

Mathematical formulation of the error in the wavelet domain.

Step 01

Photometric error in the wavelet domain

Instead of comparing raw pixels, we compare their wavelet coefficients.

e(t) = W(I*) − W(I(t))
W wavelet transform I* desired image I(t) current image
Step 02  ·  LWT specificity

Energy normalization factor

The LWT introduces a normalization factor K to preserve numerical accuracy despite the absence of expensive multiplications.

eLWT = K · ( WL(I*) − WL(I(t)) )
