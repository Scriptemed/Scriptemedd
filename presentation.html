Problem Statement

The limitations of pure Photometric Visual Servoing (PVS)

Luminosity Dependence

Strong reliance on the assumption of constant brightness — rarely valid in real-world conditions

Glare Attacks

High vulnerability to dynamic lighting changes, reflections, and sudden illumination shifts

Convergence Issues

Performance degradation when operating far from the desired pose due to large pixel errors

No Spatial Localization

Fourier Transform-based analysis lacks spatial information for non-stationary signals (images)

Visual Servoing: State of the Art

IBVS (Image-Based)

Control law computed directly in the image space using geometric features (points, lines, moments)

PBVS (Pose-Based)

Estimates the 3D pose of the object and computes control in Cartesian space

PVS — Direct / Photometric Approach

Uses raw pixel intensities directly — no feature extraction needed. Minimizes photometric error between current and desired image. Simple but sensitive to illumination.

Frequency Analysis & Its Limits

  • Classical Fourier Transform provides global frequency information but loses all spatial detail
  • Cannot localize where a frequency component occurs in the image
  • Non-stationary signals (like images) need time-frequency or space-frequency representations
  • This motivates the use of Wavelet Transforms which provide both spatial and frequency resolution

The Wavelet Solution: DWT & LWT

DWT (Discrete Wavelet Transform)

Separates global trends (illumination via approximation coefficients) from structural details (contours via detail coefficients) at multiple scales

LWT (Lifting Wavelet Transform)

Spatial optimization via 3 steps: Split, Predict, Update. Avoids complex multiplications for real-time computation

Proposed Approach: DWT-PVS & LWT-PVS

  • Compute the photometric error in the wavelet domain instead of raw pixel space
  • LWT specificity: integration of an energy normalization factor to preserve precision across decomposition levels
  • Interaction matrix adapted for wavelet coefficients — maintaining the visual servoing control framework
  • Multi-resolution error allows coarse alignment first, then fine refinement

Control Architecture & Smart Switch

  • Control loop implemented in ViSP framework — 25 FPS, sampling period of 0.04s
  • Smart Switch mechanism: dynamically transitions from coarse approximation (Low-Low) to fine details (High frequencies)
  • Velocity saturation applied to guarantee robot safety during large motions
  • Strategy ensures fast convergence from far poses while maintaining sub-pixel precision near the target

Key Advantage

The Smart Switch combines the large convergence domain of low-frequency servoing with the high precision of high-frequency servoing — achieving the best of both worlds.
