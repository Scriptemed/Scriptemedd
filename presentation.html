Problem Statement

Limitations of Pure Photometric Visual Servoing (PVS)

  • Strong dependency on the brightness constancy assumption
  • High vulnerability to dynamic lighting variations
  • Susceptibility to Glare Attacks — sudden brightness changes that destabilize the control loop

⚠️ These limitations make pure PVS unreliable in real-world uncontrolled environments

Visual Servoing Concepts & Frequency Analysis

Visual Servoing Concepts

IBVS

Image-Based Visual Servoing
Control law defined directly in the image space using geometric features (points, lines)

PBVS

Pose-Based Visual Servoing
Estimates the 3D pose then computes control in Cartesian space

PVS — Photometric Visual Servoing (Direct Approach)

Directly uses pixel intensities without extracting geometric features. Simpler but sensitive to illumination changes.

Frequency Analysis & Its Limits

  • 📈 Classical tool: Fourier Transform — decomposes signals into frequency components
  • ❌ Limitation: No spatial localization for non-stationary signals
  • 📸 Images are inherently non-stationary — edges, textures, and lighting vary spatially

Key Insight: We need a transform that provides both frequency and spatial information → Wavelets

The Wavelet Solution: DWT & LWT

🌊 DWT

Discrete Wavelet Transform

  • Separates global trends (illumination)
  • Extracts structural details (contours)

⚡ LWT

Lifting Wavelet Transform

  • Spatial optimization via Split, Predict, Update
  • Avoids complex multiplications
  • Enables real-time computation

DWT-PVS & LWT-PVS Mathematical Modeling

Mathematical Modeling: DWT-PVS & LWT-PVS

  • 🔢 Computation of the photometric error in the wavelet domain
  • 📊 Image decomposed into sub-bands: approximation (Low-Low) and details (LH, HL, HH)
  • 💡 LWT Specificity: Integration of an energy normalization factor to preserve precision across scales

Error formulation: e(r) = W(I(r)) - W(I*) where W is the wavelet transform, I(r) is current image, I* is desired image
