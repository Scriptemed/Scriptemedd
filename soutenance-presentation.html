Problem Statement

Pure Photometric Visual Servoing (PVS) relies on the brightness constancy assumption, making it vulnerable to real-world illumination changes.

  • Convergence failure or divergence in real-world conditions

Direct Photometric Visual Servoing (PVS)

Uses the entire image as a feature vector. The error signal is computed directly from pixel intensity differences between the current and desired images. Elegant but fragile under non-ideal conditions.

e(t) = I(t) - I*   |   v = -λ LI+ (I - I*)

Frequency Analysis & Its Limits

The Fourier Transform provides global frequency content but loses all spatial localization -- critical for non-stationary signals like images.

Fourier Limitation

Cannot localize where a frequency component exists in space

Need for Wavelets

Multi-resolution analysis provides both spatial and frequency information simultaneously

The Wavelet Solution: DWT & LWT

DWT (Discrete Wavelet Transform)

Separates global trends (illumination) from structural details (edges, textures). Produces 4 sub-bands: LL, LH, HL, HH

CWT

Continuous version with redundant representation -- high computational cost, not suitable for real-time

LWT (Lifting Wavelet Transform)

Spatial optimization via in-place computation. No complex multiplications required for real-time performance

Mathematical Modeling: DWT-PVS & LWT-PVS

The photometric error is computed in the wavelet domain, providing robustness to illumination-induced noise.

DWT-PVS Error Formulation

ew = W(I(r(t))) - W(I*) = Iw(t) - Iw*

Where W denotes the wavelet transform operator applied to the image

LWT-PVS with Energy Normalization

elwt = (1/NE) · [L(I(r(t))) - L(I*)]
NE = √(Σ |ck|²)    (Energy normalization factor)

Energy normalization preserves accuracy while compensating for coefficient magnitude differences between levels

Control Law

vc = -λ · Lw+ · ew   |   Lw = ∂Iw/∂r · Lr

The interaction matrix Lw relates wavelet coefficient variations to camera velocity
